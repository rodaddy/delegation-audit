# Worked example 2 -- Mercer & Co (the refusal doing its job)

**Who:** Priya Mercer, owner, Mercer & Co. Six-person marketing agency,
Huntington. Self-installed the kit; her own branding on the page.

**Why this example exists:** three of her tasks were too vague to grade, and
what the kit did about that is the most valuable thing in the session. If you
read only one example, read this one.

**Everything below is invented for illustration.**

---

## Stage 1 -- Task dump (abridged)

Priya dumped eleven items:

> "Client calls. Reviewing the team's work. Handled the Brennan situation.
> New business stuff. Invoicing. Wrote the case study. Fixed the deck for
> Thursday. Hiring -- we're looking for a designer. Did the numbers for last
> month. Bunch of admin. Sorted out the freelancer thing."

---

## Stage 2 -- The rate gate

> **AI:** What do you actually make an hour?

> **Priya:** I bill at 225 but that's not what I make.

> **AI:** Right -- the number I want is what the business pays you, divided by
> the hours you really work. What did you take out last year, and how many
> hours a week?

> **Priya:** 140 and about 55.

> **AI:** 140,000 / (55 x 48) = **$53 an hour**. That's the number every hour
> on this page is priced at. It's well under your bill rate, which is exactly
> why an hour you spend on admin is expensive twice.

Recorded: `Effective hourly rate: $53`.

---

## Stage 3 -- The refusals, in full

### Refusal 1 -- "New business stuff"

> **AI:** How many hours a week is "new business stuff"?

> **Priya:** I don't know. A lot? It varies.

> **AI:** I can't grade that one, and I'm not going to guess at it. "New
> business stuff" could be three different jobs that get three different
> answers: writing proposals, taking intro calls, or chasing people who went
> quiet. Which of those eats the most of your week?

> **Priya:** ...Proposals. Definitely proposals.

> **AI:** Then let's grade proposals and leave the rest for now. How many
> hours a week on proposals?

> **Priya:** Four. Sometimes six if two land at once.

Result: **"New business stuff" was NOT graded.** It went to the un-finished
queue with the question "which part -- proposals, intro calls, or chasing?"
**Writing proposals** was graded separately at 4.0 hrs/wk.

Note what did NOT happen: the AI did not estimate "a lot" as five hours and
move on. The un-graded parent stayed on the page as an open item.

### Refusal 2 -- "Bunch of admin"

> **AI:** Hours on "bunch of admin"?

> **Priya:** Honestly no idea. It's the stuff between things.

> **AI:** That's the hardest kind to see, and it's usually where the hours
> hide. I can't price it as one thing though. Next week, when you catch
> yourself doing "admin," write down what the actual task was -- I'd bet
> there are three repeating ones and they're all gradable. Want me to put it
> on the page as open, so it doesn't get forgotten?

> **Priya:** Yes. That's annoyingly accurate.

Result: **un-finished queue**, with the question "what were the three things
you actually did the last three times this happened?"

### Refusal 3 -- "Handled the Brennan situation"

> **AI:** How often does something like the Brennan situation come up, and how
> long does it take?

> **Priya:** That one was a one-off. Client relationship blew up.

> **AI:** Then I'm going to leave it off the audit rather than average it into
> a weekly number. A one-off isn't a weekly cost, and pretending it is would
> inflate everything else on the page. If relationship rescues start being a
> pattern, that's a different conversation and a real one.

Result: **un-finished queue**, marked as a one-off rather than a vagueness
problem. Nothing was invented for it.

---

## Stage 4 -- What did grade

| Task | Hrs/wk | Breaks if wrong | Judgment | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Writing proposals | 4.0 | Loses the pitch | mixed: strategy hers, the document not | AI drafts, you approve |
| Reviewing the team's work | 3.5 | Bad work ships | hers | Stays yours |
| Client calls | 3.0 | Relationship damage | hers | Stays yours |
| Monthly numbers | 2.0 | Real, financial | a judgment (bookkeeper) | Hand down, AI assists |
| Fixing the Thursday deck | 2.0 | Embarrassing, recoverable | nobody's | AI carries it |
| Writing the case study | 2.0 | Nothing really | nobody's | AI carries it |
| Invoicing | 1.5 | Cash flow | nobody's | AI drafts, you approve |
| Hiring -- designer search | 1.5 | Wrong hire, expensive | hers | Stays yours |
| Freelancer scheduling | 1.0 | Missed deadline | a judgment (account lead) | Hand down, AI assists |

---

## Stage 5 -- The arithmetic, shown

```
AI carries it          = 2.0 (case study) + 2.0 (deck)          = 4.0
AI drafts, you approve = 4.0 (proposals) + 1.5 (invoicing)      = 5.5
Hand down, AI assists  = 2.0 (numbers) + 1.0 (freelancers)      = 3.0
Stays yours            = 3.5 + 3.0 + 1.5                        = 8.0

reclaimed hours per week  = 4.0 + 5.5 + (3.0 / 2) = 11.0
reclaimed hours per month = 11.0 x 4.33 = 47.6
monthly value             = 47.6 x $53 = $2,523
total hours reviewed      = 20.5
```

Three tasks -- "new business stuff," "bunch of admin," and the Brennan
situation -- are counted in **nothing**. Not in the 20.5, not in the 11.0.

> **Priya:** So the real number is higher than 11, because admin isn't in
> there at all.

> **AI:** Almost certainly. That's why the open list is on the page. I'd
> rather hand you a number you can defend than a bigger one you can't.

---

## What the finished page says

Headline: 11.0 hours a week, split into 9.5 carried by software and 1.5 moved
to a person; 47.6 a month; $2,523. **Un-finished queue: three items**, each in
Priya's own words with the one question that unblocks it.
See `audit.html`.

The open section is the part she photographed and sent to her business
partner.
