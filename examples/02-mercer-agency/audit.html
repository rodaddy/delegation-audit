What moves down, and to whom

Each of these already needs a judgment -- just not yours. It moves to the person who owns that outcome, and they get an assist that makes it a bigger job than it was, not a smaller one.

Monthly numbers → the bookkeeper

She already owns the outcome; you were doing the assembly. AI pulls the month together in the shape she wants, she runs it, and you read one summary instead of building the spreadsheet.

Freelancer scheduling → the account lead

This is the one worth a conversation. Right now you are the routing table for every freelancer, which means the account lead owns the client's deadline without owning the resource that meets it. Giving her the schedule -- with AI tracking who is booked, what is due, and what has slipped -- makes her job whole. You see anything at risk the same day.

What stays yours

These need your relationships, your taste, or your read on a person. This is what the eleven hours are for -- more time inside the pitches and the review, which is what clients are actually buying.

  • Reviewing the team's work -- your standard is the product; nobody else can apply it yet.
  • Client calls -- these are relationships, not transactions.
  • The strategy inside a proposal -- AI assembles the document, but the thinking is why you win.
  • Hiring -- at six people, one wrong hire is a year of drag.

How these numbers were figured

Every hour above came from Priya's own answers -- nothing was estimated, and the three open items are counted in nothing at all. Hand-down tasks count at half, because handing work over and reading the summary still takes real time. A month is counted as 4.33 weeks. The $53 rate is Priya's own figure: $140,000 of owner pay divided by 55 hours a week across 48 weeks.

Where to start

  1. This week: track the admin. Every time you catch yourself doing "admin," write the actual task down. Three entries is enough to grade it, and it is probably the biggest number on this page.
  2. The proposal document. Four hours a week, and the strategy stays yours -- feed the last three winning proposals in as the pattern.
  3. Give the account lead the freelancer schedule. The conversation is the work; the tracking is the easy part.
  4. Then the deck fixes and invoicing. Small hours, but they are the ones that eat a Thursday.

Run this audit again in a quarter against the same list -- the second one shows what actually moved, and what crept back.
