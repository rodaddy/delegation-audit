# Worked example 1 -- Harbor Electric

**Who:** Dave Moretti, owner, Harbor Electric. 11 employees, residential and
light commercial, Suffolk County. Delivered inside a coaching session; the
coach's branding is on the finished page.

**Everything below is invented for illustration.** No real business, person,
or number appears in this file.

---

## Stage 1 -- Task dump

> **Dave:** task dump

> **AI:** Task dump active. Go -- everything you actually did, in any order.

> **Dave:** OK. Wrote up quotes. Chased the supply house about the panel
> order. Did payroll. Went out to the Hendricks job because Mike called about
> the meter location. Answered the phone about eight hundred times. Scheduled
> next week. Invoiced Friday. Argued with an insurance certificate thing.
> Wrote the ad for the apprentice position. Did the safety meeting.

> **AI:** Got all nine. Keep going -- open your calendar for last week, what's
> in there we haven't named?

> **Dave:** The Tuesday walkthrough at the church. And I went back to the
> Hendricks job Thursday for the same thing.

> **AI:** What did you do that you were annoyed you were doing?

> **Dave:** All of the phone. And the quotes, honestly. It's the same six
> paragraphs every time with a different number.

> **AI:** What happens after hours or on the weekend that isn't on any
> calendar?

> **Dave:** Sunday night I do next week's schedule. And I read every email
> that came in Saturday so nothing sits.

> **AI:** That's fourteen. Ready for me to grade them one at a time? It's one
> question at a time and takes about fifteen minutes.

> **Dave:** Go.

---

## Stage 2 -- The rate gate

> **AI:** One number before we start, and it's yours, not a market average:
> what do you actually make an hour? If it's easier -- what did the business
> pay you last year, and how many hours a week do you really work? I'll do
> the math.

> **Dave:** Took about 185 out last year. I'm there 60 hours easy, plus the
> Sunday stuff.

> **AI:** Call it 63 then. 185,000 divided by (63 x 48 weeks) = **$61 an
> hour**. That's your effective rate -- it's lower than you'd guess, which is
> the point: every hour you spend on typing costs you an hour you could spend
> selling. Use 61, or adjust the 48 weeks?

> **Dave:** 61 is fine. Depressing but fine.

Recorded: `Effective hourly rate: $61` (185,000 / (63 x 48)).

---

## Stage 3 -- Grading, one task at a time

Only the first four are transcribed in full; the rest follow the identical
four-question shape.

### Task 1 -- "Wrote up quotes"

> **AI:** How many hours a week does writing quotes actually take you?
> (My guess from what you said: it sounds like most of a day.)

> **Dave:** Six. Maybe seven in a good week, which is worse.

> **AI:** What breaks if a quote goes out wrong?

> **Dave:** If the number's wrong I eat it. If the scope is wrong we fight
> about it later. It's real money.

> **AI:** Does that need YOUR judgment, or just A judgment? Specifically -- is
> it the pricing call that needs you, or the writing around it?

> **Dave:** The number needs me. The six paragraphs around it do not.

> **AI:** Then this splits, and that's the finding. The writing is
> `AI carries it`; the number is `Stays yours`. Concretely: AI drafts the
> whole quote from the job notes with the price left blank, you fill the
> number and send. What's the writing worth -- of the six hours, how much is
> the typing?

> **Dave:** Five of it, easy.

Recorded: **Writing up quotes (the document)** -- 5.0 hrs/wk, real money if
wrong, nobody's judgment on the prose -> `AI drafts, you approve`.
**Pricing the quote** -- 1.0 hrs/wk, real money, Dave's judgment ->
`Stays yours`.

### Task 2 -- "Answered the phone about eight hundred times"

> **AI:** How many hours a week is the phone?

> **Dave:** No idea. It's constant.

> **AI:** Let's bound it. Roughly how many calls a day, and how long is a
> typical one?

> **Dave:** Twenty-five, thirty. Most are two minutes -- "are you coming
> Tuesday."

> **AI:** So call it 28 calls at 2 minutes = about an hour a day, 5 hours a
> week, plus the handful of long ones. Fair?

> **Dave:** Six with the long ones.

> **AI:** What breaks if a call is handled wrong?

> **Dave:** Depends entirely. "Are you coming Tuesday" -- nothing. A new
> customer pricing a service upgrade -- that's a job I lose.

> **AI:** Same split as the quotes then. The "are you coming Tuesday" calls --
> does answering those need YOUR judgment, or just a judgment?

> **Dave:** Anybody with the schedule in front of them.

> **AI:** And who owns the schedule?

> **Dave:** Nobody, really. Whoever's in the office. Usually Ang does it when
> she's here.

> **AI:** Before I grade it -- does this one happen on a screen, or out in the
> world? Because a phone call is a live voice, and software doesn't make phone
> calls. Is there a list, a write-up, or a follow-up hanging off it?

> **Dave:** ...Yeah. Half of it is me texting people "we're running late" or
> "confirmed for Tuesday" after. That part's typing.

> **AI:** Then let's split it, because they're two different answers. Of the
> four and a half hours, how much is you actually on the phone, and how much is
> the texting and confirming?

> **Dave:** Call it three on the phone, an hour and a half of the texting.

> **AI:** That's a finding on its own -- I'll put it on the page. Angela owning
> the schedule is a bigger job than catching phones ad hoc, and the
> confirmations are the part software genuinely takes off both of you.

Recorded as two lines:
**Routine scheduling calls (on the phone)** -- 3.0 hrs/wk, in the world,
small and reversible, a judgment, owner is Angela (informally) ->
`Hand down, no AI`, with "no formal owner yet" flagged. **No software is
involved in this one and the page says so** -- Angela makes the calls, and a
summary comes back to Dave.
**Confirmations and running-late texts** -- 1.5 hrs/wk, on a screen, small and
reversible, nobody's judgment -> `AI carries it`.
**New-customer calls** -- 1.5 hrs/wk, in the world, a lost job if wrong,
Dave's judgment -> `Stays yours`.

### Task 3 -- "Sunday night I do next week's schedule"

> **AI:** Hours?

> **Dave:** Two and a half. Every Sunday.

> **AI:** What breaks if it's wrong?

> **Dave:** Crews show up to the wrong address. Happened twice this year, cost
> me a whole morning both times.

> **AI:** Does it need YOUR judgment?

> **Dave:** ...Some of it. Who goes where matters, I know which guys handle
> which customers. But the actual laying it out on the grid, no.

> **AI:** `AI drafts, you approve`: AI lays out the grid from the job list and
> last week's assignments, you move the names around and approve it. My guess
> is that turns 2.5 hours into 30 minutes on Sunday night.

> **Dave:** If that's real, that alone is worth it.

Recorded: **Weekly scheduling** -- 2.5 hrs/wk, crews to wrong addresses if
wrong, a mix -> `AI drafts, you approve`.

### Task 4 -- "Argued with an insurance certificate thing"

> **AI:** How often is that, and how long?

> **Dave:** Every couple weeks. An hour when it happens.

> **AI:** So 0.5 hrs/wk. What breaks if it's wrong?

> **Dave:** GC won't let us on site. That's a whole day gone.

> **AI:** Whose judgment?

> **Dave:** Nobody's. It's a form. It's the same form.

Recorded: **Certificate of insurance requests** -- 0.5 hrs/wk, day lost if
wrong, nobody's judgment -> `AI drafts, you approve` (real consequence, so it
gets a look before it goes).

## Stage 4 -- The arithmetic, shown

```
AI carries it          = 0.5 (job ad) + 1.5 (Saturday email)
                       + 1.5 (confirmation texts)             = 3.5
AI drafts, you approve = 5.0 (quote writing) + 2.5 (scheduling)
                       + 0.5 (COIs) + 1.5 (invoicing)
                       + 0.5 (order-status list)              = 10.0
Hand down, AI assists  = 1.0 (payroll)                        = 1.0
Hand down, no AI       = 3.0 (routine calls)
                       + 1.0 (supply house calls)             = 4.0
Stays yours            = 1.0 + 1.5 + 2.0 + 2.0 + 1.0          = 7.5

reclaimed hours per week  = 3.5 + 10.0 + ((1.0 + 4.0) / 2) = 16.0
  of that, software carries                                = 13.5
  of that, a person carries                                =  2.5
reclaimed hours per month = 16.0 x 4.33 = 69.3
monthly value             = 69.3 x $61 = $4,227
total hours reviewed      = 26.0   (Dave works 63 -- see the note on the page)
```

> **AI:** Sixteen hours a week, out of the twenty-six we put a name to -- and
> you told me you work sixty-three, so this is the part of your week you could
> account for, not the whole thing. At your own $61, that's about **$4,227 a
> month of your time** going into work that never needed your judgment.
> Thirteen and a half of those hours software actually carries; the other two
> and a half move to Angela. Nobody leaves -- Angela ends up owning the
> schedule and the supply house instead of catching phones, and you get Sunday
> night back.

> **Dave:** Build it. I want to show my wife.

## What the finished page says

See `audit.html` in this folder. Headline: 16.0 hours a week (13.5 carried by
software, 2.5 moving to Angela), 69.3 a month, $4,227. Un-finished queue:
empty for this one -- Dave described everything concretely. (Example 2 shows
what happens when they don't.)

Two of Dave's lines came back `Hand down, no AI` -- the scheduling calls and
the supply-house calls. Those are the ones worth looking at if you work with
your hands: the kit did not pretend software would make a phone call, it found
the typing wrapped around the call and took that instead.
