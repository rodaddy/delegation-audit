What this says

Of the nineteen and a half hours we walked through, nine and a quarter go to work that never needed your judgment -- certificates, ID cards, chasing signatures, and reconciling commissions. That is a full week out of every month. The hours go back to renewals, quoting, and sitting with a client through a claim, which is the entire reason people stay with a local agent instead of an app.

The one line A full week of every month goes to paperwork that a draft-and-approve loop can carry.

Where the work goes

Nothing here removes a person from the agency. Every line moves a task: down to whoever owns that outcome, with AI carrying the mechanical part -- and a short summary coming back to you for approval. You keep every decision. You stop doing the typing.

What moves down, and to whom

This one already needs a judgment -- just not yours. It moves to the person who owns that outcome, and she gets an assist that makes it a bigger job than it was, not a smaller one.

Adding and removing vehicles → your assistant

Today she types what you dictate, which means you are still doing the thinking on every endorsement. Give her the endorsements outright, with AI checking each one for a coverage gap before it goes and flagging anything unusual for you. She stops being a typist and starts owning a piece of service the clients feel directly. A weekly summary comes back to you, and anything that looks like a real gap comes up the same day. This is the line to talk to her about first -- it is a step up in what she does, and it should be presented that way.

What stays yours

These need your license, your relationships, and thirty years of knowing what a policy actually does when something goes wrong. This is what the nine hours a week are for -- more renewals worked properly, more quoting, more time on the day a claim happens.

  • Quoting new business -- the coverage recommendation carries your license.
  • Claims hand-holding -- nobody remembers the certificate; everybody remembers the claim.

Where to start

  1. This week: certificates. Three hours, the most repetitive thing on the list, and the clients who need them need them fast. Feed the last ten as the pattern.
  2. Talk to your assistant about the endorsements. The conversation is the work; the coverage-gap check is the easy part.
  3. Then the signature chasing. Small hours, but it is the one that keeps a policy from binding on time.
  4. Break "servicing" apart. Next week, note what each caller actually wanted. That list is the rest of this audit.

Run this audit again in a quarter against the same list -- the second one shows what actually moved, and what crept back.
