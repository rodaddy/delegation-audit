# Worked example 3 -- Alvarez Insurance (the hours-only path)

**Who:** Ruth Alvarez, solo licensed producer with one part-time assistant,
Bay Shore. Personal lines and small commercial.

**Why this example exists:** Ruth would not state what she makes an hour. The
kit does not guess, does not substitute an industry average, and does not
print a single dollar figure anywhere on the page. The audit is still worth
handing over.

**Everything below is invented for illustration.**

---

## The rate gate, declined

> **AI:** One number before we start, and it's yours, not a market average:
> what do you actually make an hour? If it's easier -- what did the business
> pay you last year, and how many hours a week do you really work? I'll do
> the math.

> **Ruth:** I'd rather not put that in writing. My son-in-law is going to read
> this thing.

> **AI:** Completely fine. Then the page comes out in hours only -- no dollar
> figures anywhere on it. I'm not going to plug in an industry average,
> because a number you didn't give me isn't worth anything to you. You'll see
> hours a week and hours a month, and you can do the math in your head.

> **Ruth:** That's better anyway.

Recorded: `Effective hourly rate: not set`.

**What this changes on the page:** the third headline stat is deleted
entirely, the monthly-value line is gone from the arithmetic block, and one
plain line appears under the headline numbers. No dollar sign appears in the
finished file.

---

## The dump and the grading (abridged)

Ruth named ten tasks. Two hit the refusal.

### Refusal -- "Servicing"

> **AI:** How many hours a week is "servicing"?

> **Ruth:** That's the whole job, honey.

> **AI:** Then it's too big for me to grade as one thing. Break it for me:
> when a client calls in, what are the three things they most often want?

> **Ruth:** Certificate. ID card. Or they're adding a car.

> **AI:** Those grade separately, and I bet they get three different answers.
> Let's take them one at a time.

Result: **"Servicing" was NOT graded.** It went to the un-finished queue.
Three tasks were split out of it and graded individually -- certificates, ID
cards, and vehicle adds.

### Refusal -- "Dealing with the carriers"

> **AI:** Hours?

> **Ruth:** Depends who's being difficult that week.

> **AI:** I can't price "depends." Was there one this week, and how long did
> it take?

> **Ruth:** Two hours with one of them. Some weeks none.

> **AI:** That's a range, not a rate, and averaging it would put a number on
> this page you couldn't defend. It goes on the open list with the question:
> over the last month, how many of these were there? Four answers and it's
> gradable.

Result: **un-finished queue.**

## The arithmetic, shown -- hours only

```
AI carries it          = 2.0 (ID cards)                          = 2.0
AI drafts, you approve = 3.0 (certificates) + 1.5 (signatures)
                       + 1.5 (commission reconciliation)         = 6.0
Hand down, AI assists  = 2.5 (vehicle adds)                      = 2.5
Stays yours            = 4.0 + 3.0 + 2.0                         = 9.0

reclaimed hours per week  = 2.0 + 6.0 + (2.5 / 2) = 9.25
reclaimed hours per month = 9.25 x 4.33 = 40.1
total hours reviewed      = 19.5

monthly value: NOT COMPUTED -- no hourly rate was given.
```

> **AI:** Nine and a quarter hours a week, forty a month. That's a full week
> of every month going into certificates, ID cards, and chasing signatures.
> Nothing on this page is priced, because you didn't give me a rate -- you
> know better than I do what a week of your time is worth.

> **Ruth:** A week a month. Put that on the front.

## What the finished page says

Headline: three stats -- 9.25 hours a week, split into 8.0 carried by software
and 1.25 moved to a person, plus 40.1 a month. One line under them: "Hours only:
no hourly rate was set, so nothing here is priced."
Un-finished queue: two items. See `audit.html`.

Note the hand-down: the vehicle adds move to Ruth's part-time assistant, who
goes from typing what Ruth dictates to owning endorsements with AI checking
the coverage-gap risk for her. Ruth reads a weekly summary. That is a bigger
job for the assistant, not a smaller one.
